Quick recommendation: for most CSE students, buy a Windows laptop with a recent Ryzen 5/7 or Intel Core 5/7 H/P/U-class CPU, 16GB RAM minimum (32GB if running VMs/containers/ML), and a 512GB NVMe SSD. RAM: 8GB vs 16GB vs 32GB

  • 8GB: avoid for a new 4-year CSE purchase unless budget is extremely tight and upgrade is confirmed.
  • 16GB: recommended starting point for VS Code, browsers, IDEs and databases.
  • 32GB: ideal for multiple VMs, Docker, Android Studio, larger datasets and local AI tools.
  • Check if RAM is soldered, has a free slot, or is fully replaceable.

SSD: 512GB vs 1TB

  • 512GB NVMe: sensible minimum for OS, IDEs, code, projects and files.
  • 1TB NVMe: better for Android emulators, virtual machines, datasets and games.
  • Check whether a second M.2 slot exists and whether the SSD is replaceable.
  • SSD is storage; RAM is working memory. They are not interchangeable.

What the laptop series mean

Series names indicate the laptop's purpose, but individual model configurations still matter.

Lenovo

  • IdeaPad Slim: mainstream student / everyday laptops.
  • ThinkBook: productivity and small-business features, often good ports.
  • ThinkPad E/T/X: business-focused keyboards, durability and service features.
  • LOQ: entry gaming / performance with discrete GPUs.
  • Legion: higher-end gaming and creator performance.

HP

  • HP 15 / 14: mainstream everyday and student models.
  • Pavilion / OmniBook: mainstream-to-premium consumer productivity, depending on model.
  • ProBook / EliteBook: business-focused security, support and build.
  • Victus: gaming / GPU performance at more accessible prices.
  • OMEN: higher-end gaming and creator workloads.

Dell

  • Dell 14 / 15: mainstream daily-use laptops.
  • Inspiron: consumer productivity range; model generation matters.
  • Latitude: business and enterprise range.
  • Precision: workstation class for specialist professional workloads.
  • Alienware: premium gaming / high-performance systems.

ASUS

  • Vivobook: mainstream student and productivity laptops.
  • Zenbook: premium thin-and-light laptops, often better displays.
  • ExpertBook: business-focused range.
  • TUF Gaming: gaming/performance with discrete graphics.
  • ROG: premium gaming and creator performance.

Acer & MSI

  • Acer Aspire: mainstream student and everyday use.
  • Acer Swift: thin-and-light productivity.
  • Acer Nitro / Predator: gaming and GPU workloads.
  • MSI Modern / Prestige: productivity and creator portability.
  • MSI Thin / Katana / Raider: gaming ranges; compare thermals and service availability.

Apple & Samsung

  • MacBook Air: portable, quiet, excellent battery; suitable for many programming paths.
  • MacBook Pro: sustained performance, display and ports for demanding macOS workflows.
  • Samsung Galaxy Book: thin-and-light Windows productivity and ecosystem features.
  • Confirm course software compatibility before choosing macOS or ARM Windows.

Pre-purchase checklist

  • 16GB RAM minimum; 32GB preferred for heavy VM/AI workloads.
  • 512GB PCIe NVMe SSD minimum; 1TB if budget permits.
  • IPS/OLED display; aim for 300 nits and good colour coverage.
  • Backlit keyboard, USB-C charging/DisplayPort and HDMI are useful.
  • Check RAM is upgradeable before buying.
  • Check 1-year onsite warranty / extended warranty options.
  • For gaming GPU laptops, compare RTX model, VRAM and GPU TGP.
  • Check return policy, GST invoice, seller and sealed-box serial number.
